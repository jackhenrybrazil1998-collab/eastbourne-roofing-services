Detailed advice

Understanding the work before you book

Leaves, moss and debris can build up around gutter outlets and downpipe mouths. Overflowing water can dampen exterior walls, damage paintwork and conceal other roofline problems. Cleaning should therefore include a look for visibly loose joints and sections that do not drain as intended.

Suitable access is important: different building heights and roof shapes call for different cleaning methods. A cleaning visit is not the same as a full leak investigation or survey of concealed drainage runs.

If the same gutter frequently blocks, ask whether overhanging trees, roof moss, outlet design or defective alignment contribute. We can discuss any repairs separately rather than assuming cleaning will cure every problem.

What to send when requesting an inspection

Send your East Sussex postcode, whether the issue is urgent, a brief description of the affected area and, if you have them, photos taken safely from the ground or indoors. Tell us about previous repairs or any existing workmanship guarantee. We will discuss next steps and whether an on-site visit is required.

How we approach gutter cleaning

Step 1

Inspect accessible sections for leaves, moss, debris and obvious damaged fittings.

Step 2

Clear gutters using an appropriate safe access method and check outlets where possible.

Step 3
